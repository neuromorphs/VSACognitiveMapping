ASTM · anisotropy results · 31 July 2026

Isotropy predicts capacity. It does not predict a good key.

Four independent experiments run today on Spot data, all measuring the same two things — how anisotropic a representation is, and how well it actually retrieves. They disagree, consistently and in every direction. That dissociation is the result; the rest of this page is the evidence for it, including one hypothesis it falsifies and one claim of my own it retracts.

1 · The headline

Every point below is the same measurement. There is no curve through them.

The x-axis is IsoScore — 0 is maximally anisotropic, 1 is perfectly isotropic, and it is rotation-invariant, so it measures the shape of the covariance spectrum rather than an accident of basis. The y-axis is class-level top-1 retrieval: give the memory one object crop, exclude every neighbour within 300 frames, ask whether the nearest remaining crop is the same class. Chance is 0.312.

If isotropy were what made a key good, this would be a rising curve. It is a cloud. The four most isotropic points on the plot (IsoScore = 1.000, fully whitened) sit in the middle of the retrieval range. The best retrieval on the plot belongs to one of the least isotropic representations. The worst belongs to something in between.

encoder (same boxes) isotropisation rung (same encoder) scene representation (frame vs crop)

Four experimental designs, one plot. Each varies a different thing — the encoder, the whitening treatment, the environment, the unit of representation — and each produces points that break any monotone reading. Retrieval spans 0.435 to 0.866 while IsoScore spans 0.00002 to 1.000, with no alignment between them.

What this does and does not say. It does not say isotropy is useless. The capacity argument is untouched: bundling crosstalk depends on the covariance spectrum, and that is a claim about how many items you can superpose before readout fails.

It says isotropy is not sufficient, and is the wrong thing to optimise on its own. A perfectly isotropic encoding of the wrong referent retrieves nothing. Capacity and key quality are separate axes, and the paper should claim the trade rather than the direction.

2 · Four encoders, identical boxes

The ViT is the best-behaved. The untrained net collapses to one direction.

Every encoder saw the same 9,056 boxes, read back from the same CSV, so the encoder is the only variable. DINOv2 is preprocessed exactly as the upstream init-am pipeline does it — square bilinear resize to 224, ImageNet normalisation — rather than the HuggingFace processor's shortest-edge resize plus centre crop, which silently slices content off tall boxes.

Liang et al. (NeurIPS 2022) replicates to three decimals confirmed

Liang reports that untrained networks already show the cone effect, with random-init ResNet around 0.99 mean pairwise cosine. Measured here: +0.999, with an effective rank of 1.0 out of 2048 — the entire representation collapsed onto a single direction. Training then reduces anisotropy dramatically, 0.999 down to 0.332. Extreme anisotropy is the architectural default, not something training creates.

Godey et al. (EACL 2024) does not hold here — it inverts contradicted

Godey reports that anisotropy tracks self-attention: ViTs and transformers are anisotropic at every layer, CNNs largely are not. In this setting the ViT is the best-behaved trained encoder (IsoScore 0.0454, the highest here) and the most anisotropic trained encoder is a CNN — YOLOv8n at 0.883.

What predicts anisotropy in this table is the training objective, not the architecture. YOLO's "embedding" is a detection head's penultimate feature that was never asked to be a descriptor; ResNet-50 and DINOv2 both optimise global discriminative representations. That is a testable reframing, and it is the opposite of the prediction.

This costs us something, and it should be said first in the paper. A better backbone buys better isotropy and better retrieval together. So "why not just use a good encoder instead of whitening?" is now an obvious reviewer question.

The 365× and 22× crosstalk figures were measured on YOLO features — the worst encoder in this table, which inflates the apparent value of whitening relative to a competent descriptor. Re-measure on DINOv2 before quoting any multiplier. What survives: even the best encoder here sits at IsoScore 0.045 against 1.0, so the correction still matters — but its magnitude is encoder-dependent and has to be reported that way.

3 · The rogue-dimension test

This is not the anisotropy the NLP literature describes

Timkey & van Schijndel (EMNLP 2021) showed transformer anisotropy is mostly the work of one to five rogue dimensions: remove five and the mean cosine collapses, and the cheapest effective fix is per-dimension standardisation. If our features behaved the same way, the story would be "known anisotropy, applied to VSAs" — and the fix would be free.

The decomposition is exact rather than sampled. With unit-normalised vectors, each dimension's additive contribution to the mean off-diagonal cosine has a closed form, and the contributions sum to the measured mean.

Cumulative share of the mean off-diagonal cosine, by dimensions ranked most-contributing first. The NLP markers are single dimensions carrying 76–99% of the effect. Every vision encoder tested here — CNN, ViT, trained and untrained — needs 46 to 185 dimensions to reach half.

The untrained network is the cleanest separation of the two phenomena. It has an effective rank of 1.0 — maximally low-rank, everything on one direction — while its top coordinate carries only 1.3% of the mean cosine, and it needs 185 dimensions to reach half.

A dense shared direction is not a coordinate-aligned spike. Deleting dimensions cannot fix it, which is exactly why Timkey's cheap fix does not transfer, and why the effective-rank framing is the one that applies.

4 · The isotropisation ladder

Whitening buys isotropy by spending discriminability

Thirteen rungs on one encoder's crops, each scored on both axes. Centring alone collapses the mean cosine from 0.883 to 0.007 at essentially no retrieval cost — most of the raw figure is a shared mean offset, and it is free to remove. What centring does not touch is the spectrum: effective rank stays at 6.7. That is the part that costs.

All four whitened rungs reach IsoScore 1.000 by construction. They differ only in how many directions get equalised — and retrieval falls monotonically with that count, because the extra directions being promoted to equal footing are nuisance rather than signal.

The control rung works. A random orthogonal map cannot change any cosine, and it reproduces raw exactly on every rotation-invariant metric — mean cosine, effective rank and IsoScore all identical to twelve decimal places. The implementation is sound.

Per-dimension z-scoring is the best rung on this axis for the anisotropic encoder: it nearly doubles IsoScore, lifts effective rank from 6.7 to 10.9, and slightly improves retrieval. It only helps where there is something to fix, though — on ResNet-50 it costs 0.787 → 0.772.

5 · The second scene

A falsified hypothesis, and a retraction

school_run1 is the scale-up sequence: 41,697 detections over 11,211 frames, 9.3× the classroom's canonical event count. Same robot, same camera, same detector, different space — which makes it the free test of whether semantic diversity drives effective rank. Diversity is measured, not assumed: class entropy and its exponential, the effective number of classes.

The prediction ran backwards falsified

The hypothesis was that our anisotropy is low-rank because few classes, so a semantically richer scene should spread features over more directions. school_run1 is measurably richer — 7.1 effective classes against 6.0, 45 distinct classes against 40 — and its effective rank is lower (4.6 against 6.7), with IsoScore lower too.

The characterisation survives: it is still spectral, and the Timkey figure is unchanged at 52 dimensions for half the mean cosine. What fails is the causal story. Class diversity does not drive effective rank, at least not in the predicted direction, so that mechanism cannot be asserted.

Retraction. Earlier today I reported, from the classroom alone, that PCA whitening hurts appearance retrieval — 0.720 → 0.623, a 0.097 drop. On school_run1 the same operation costs 0.004 (0.842 → 0.838).

The whitening penalty is scene-dependent, not a property of appearance keys. The recommendation that followed from it — cutting the language pipeline from 128 PCA components to 32 — is therefore provisional and classroom-specific. It must be re-tested per scene before being adopted as a default.

The obvious confound was checked and rejected. A 300-frame gap is 12% of the classroom's 2,478 frames but only 2.7% of school_run1's 11,211, which would make the raw comparison unfair. Re-run at a proportionally matched 1,357-frame gap, school_run1 still scores 0.842 against the classroom's 0.720. The matched-N row controls separately for sample size, since both effective rank and IsoScore move with it.

6 · The enabling change

Per-object crops, and why the frame embedding could never have worked

Until today the pipeline called model.embed(img) once per frame, so every detection in a frame shared one vector. That gap is upstream's too, not a porting artefact — init-am's DINO path embeds whole frames as well, so its YOLO-versus-DINO comparison has been running on the weaker representation for both backends.

Identical detections, identical labels, identical gap control — the only change is which vector represents a detection. Crops carry 3.4× the signal above chance at a twenty-second gap.

The structural reason, quantified. 877 of 1,210 frames contain more than one distinct class, which puts 88.6% of all detections in a frame where the vector is identical across different objects.

For those, per-object cueing is not inaccurate — it is impossible in principle. And note that frame features are more isotropic than crops (IsoScore 0.0315 against 0.0222) while retrieving far worse. That is the same dissociation again, from a fourth direction.

7 · Next steps

What these results change, in order

  1. Re-measure crosstalk on DINOv2 features before quoting any multiplier. Every whitening figure in the project — 365×, 22×, 4.6× — was measured on YOLO features, which this page shows is the worst encoder available. The multiplier is encoder-dependent, and publishing one measured on the weakest backbone is the kind of thing a reviewer with pip finds in an hour. This is cheap: the DINOv2 crops already exist.
  2. Define the crosstalk metric once, precisely. Mean cosine and IsoScore rank the encoders differently — cosine says YOLO is worst, IsoScore says ResNet-50 is — and retrieval disagrees with both. Until the paper names which quantity its headline number refers to, the headline number does not mean anything. This also permanently resolves the 22×-versus-13× ambiguity.
  3. Make the dissociation a figure, not a caveat. Four independent designs now show it. It pre-empts the obvious objection to the capacity story ("if isotropy is so good, why not maximise it?") with data rather than argument, and it sharpens the contribution from a direction into a trade-off with an optimum — which is territory the Hazy Research fact-storing result does not occupy, because a storage-only setting has no competing retrieval objective to expose.
  4. Run Gate 0 on school_run1, not the classroom. At 41,697 detections it is roughly 3× the ~13k PQ-kNN crossover, making it the first regime where the byte-budget claim can be tested rather than assumed. The classroom sits below crossover, where the honest answer is already "the exact table wins."
  5. Reconcile the event count before any figure is redrawn. Stride-1 crop extraction gives 9,056 detections where the canonical pipeline reports 4,498, largely because upstream applies --trim-stationary. N appears in every crosstalk and crossover number, so which one the paper quotes moves the exact-table crossover.
  6. Do not act on the 128 → 32 whitening change yet. It held on the classroom and vanished on school_run1. Re-test per scene first.

Still unmeasured, and load-bearing. Every retrieval number on this page is raw cosine retrieval, not retrieval through the VSA memory — and inside the memory, isotropy is what buys the crosstalk reduction. The net trade has not been measured.

Everything here is also class-level. Neither sequence has instance ground truth, so the five-identical-chairs case — the named losing scenario against a kNN vector database — remains untested.
